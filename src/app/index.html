English → Mandarin, Japanese and Korean

Rule-based drafts: each word is looked up in the dictionaries and put into the grammar of each language (word order, particles, measure words, verb endings). They are a starting point, not a fluent translation, and the studio cannot check whether a draft reads naturally: it shows only what it can check (the sentence pattern, English words it could not use, words not found) and a human translation from Tatoeba when one exists for the same English. Polite style is used for Japanese (です/ます) and Korean (해요).
Measured on 1,800 short Tatoeba sentences (600 per language): drafts matched a human translation exactly only 2% of the time in Mandarin and under 1% in Japanese and Korean, and on average shared about half their characters with the closest human translation (Mandarin 55%, Japanese 44%, Korean 41%). Treat every draft as a rough guide.

About

East Asian Languages Studio by Larry Dalton: write, read and study Mandarin Chinese, Japanese and Korean on your own computer.

  • Write: Simplified and Traditional Chinese with pinyin or zhuyin, Japanese with furigana or romaji, Korean with Revised Romanization or Hanja; horizontal or vertical; English names by sound (Larry Dalton → 拉里·道尔顿, ラリー・ドルトン, 래리 돌톤); export SVG, PNG, JPEG.
  • Translate: rule-based English → Mandarin, Japanese and Korean drafts with a word-by-word breakdown.
  • Dictionary: CC-CEDICT, JMdict and Korean Wiktionary (with kengdic for extra words), character readings from Unihan, stroke order for Chinese characters, kanji and hanja.
  • Examples: sentence pairs from Tatoeba.
  • Reader: SVGs read exactly; PNG and JPEG images matched against the studio's fonts (ruby lines are not read from images).
